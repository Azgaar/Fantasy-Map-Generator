# Battles

How a battle is fought on the battle plan. The user guide is the [Battle Simulator wiki page](../wiki/Battle-Simulator.md).

A battle is a deterministic simulation of **battalions** on a field measured in metres and fought in ticks of eight seconds. Two levels decide what happens:

1. a **general** for each side, who reads the whole field once a round, picks a **tactic**, draws the troops up and gives every battalion an **order**;
2. the **battalion** itself, which every tick weighs its order against what is around it: who threatens it, whom it can hurt, how shaken it is.

The battle type is picked by rules, and the ground details are a fixed pattern of the place on the map (`mix` in `battlefield.ts`), so the same spot always gives the same field. A side's **die** is cast once at the opening by the battle's `seed` (`castDice`) and stands to the end, unless the user sets it by hand. It multiplies the side's blows by `1 + (die − 3.5) × settings.fortune`: with the default 0.2, ×0.5 for ⚀ to ×1.5 for ⚅, ×1 on average; 0.3 at most, where ⚀ still strikes a quarter, whatever a log or a setting says; a die set by hand is a face 1 to 6. With no seed, with `fortune` 0 and in the generals' rehearsals there is no die, ×1, and the generals do not reckon with it; every number is tuned for ×1. The screen makes a seed for a new battle and keeps it through a redeploy, a restart, the exports and the battle log, so the same setup and seed always play the same battle.

## The loop

- **On setup** (`prepare`): both generals appraise, choose a tactic by scores and deploy; the defenders first, the attackers in sight of them. It is instant, so the screen has troops to show.
- **Before the first tick** (`plan`): the generals think the battle through, see [Rehearsal](#rehearsal), and the same battalions take their final places. `tick` does it itself when nobody has, so every battle is planned however it is driven; the screen does it off a timer with the start and step buttons disabled. Budget: 1.5 s: wagon lines bar everybody, and the ways round them are slow to reckon.
- **Every round** (6 ticks, 48 s, `command`): works finished in the last round stand, then each general appraises anew, sets the side's phase, moves the formation and rewrites the orders. Budget: 100 ms.
- **Every tick** (`act`): each battalion turns its order into an intent (go there, strike or shoot that foe, stand), then all move and blows are settled together.

## The general

### Appraisal

What a general knows is what a general could see: the troops in order on both sides by kind, their power and morale, the losses so far, the ground. The kind is how a battalion fights, and it comes from its unit's type, never from its speed, so a unit's numbers can be set freely (`Battalions.getKind`): **riders** are `mounted` and `aviation`, **shooters** `ranged`, **engines** `machinery` and `naval` with a shot (ships with none board as line troops), and every other type is **line** troops that fight hand to hand. In a naval and an air battle all troops are line troops. Range says only whether a battalion can shoot: riders that shoot skirmish (see [The battalion](#the-battalion)), line troops that throw still close (`closes`), and only shooters halt the formation where their shots reach. From it:

- **odds**: own power in order to the foe's, as the military screens count it;
- **fire**: the same for what shoots; **grip** for what fights hand to hand, shooters counted at half and engines and ships at a tenth (`getGrip`);
- **wings**: the foe's left, centre and right by power, and where its line ends;
- **trend**: losses of the last rounds, own to the foe's.

### Tactic

The tactic is chosen once, before the start, by scoring every tactic the battle type allows against the appraisal; the best score wins, ties go to the first. A tactic is a set of numbers a single formation builder and a single order writer read, not code of its own.

The field sets the axis of the attack, not where the regiments stood on the map (`findAxis`): a landing or a bombardment comes straight from the water (`getLandward`); any other battle runs square to the field's edges, one of four ways, scored by sea or lake under either side's ground, a river between the two sides (`crossesRiver`) and none through either line, the defenders' ground over the attackers' (full weight at 20 m, `HIGH_GROUND`) and a burg on the defenders' side. An ambush is scored by the water alone: its column has no line to face. The way the attackers came on the map (`approach`) only breaks a tie, so on a flat dry field they come from their side. The attackers form up over against the defenders' first line and face its middle (`getDefendersLine`): their shifts across are reckoned from it, not from the middle of the field.

The scores are in `chooseTactic`, the numbers of each tactic in `TACTICS`. The defenders look for their ground first (`findGround`): lines across the attack at three depths and five shifts are scored by height, by what covers their ends (water, wood, a burg; the edge of the field is no cover, so a flat field gets a centred line) and by what the attackers must cross to reach them. The slope before a line counts the more the more of the foe's power is riders, whose charge the climb slows. Defenders ready for a landing hold the water's edge (`findShore`). Every frame is scored as it will stand on the field, and loses for each place of its formation in water or beyond it, seen from its middle; attackers come nearer, at two more depths, only when water cuts the ground they came to, and never from the sea; so neither side is drawn up across a river. `settle` steps a battalion still on a ford out of it. A formation is shifted as a whole to stay on the field: places are never clamped one by one, or they would end up on each other.

The wiki's tactic diagrams (`docs/wiki/Battle-tactic-*.png`) are drawn by `npm run battle-diagrams` ([battle-diagrams.mjs](../../scripts/battle-diagrams.mjs)) from `Formations.form`, so re-run it when a formation changes; `--only <names>` redraws some, `--svg` keeps the SVGs too.

**A line that holds follows the ground** (`bend`), as `bow` bends it to a shape: every place goes ahead or back by one curve across the formation, the lines behind with the first, so nobody stands on another. Each battalion of the first line names where it would rather stand within 1.5 fronts, and goes no further ahead than 0.35 of a front for every front across from a neighbour that wishes less, so the line stays closed. A stand or a crescent bends to the brow (`getBrowOffset`): the spot highest above the slope 1.5 fronts before it, the slope weighed more the more riders the foe has, where it is a metre better than the straight line. A line at the water's edge bends to the shore (`getShoreOffset`): half a front, a battalion's depth, behind the water, so attackers coming at it are still wading the shallows when they meet it.

**Who attacks** is `side.advancing`. Attackers do unless their odds are under 0.75 or their general settled on waiting (`side.waits`), and then they take a defender's tactic. A side given an attack by hand does, at any odds. Defenders do at odds of 1.25 with no crossing to hold. A side that waits goes over to the attack when the foe is spent (odds of 1.5 with the lines at grips), outshoots it two to one while it bleeds, or has not come for six rounds (about 5 minutes) while being no stronger; not into twice its own fire, not when its general chose to wait, not from behind finished works, and never from behind a crossing until the foe runs. A side that waits and holds no works goes over at once, at odds of 1 or better, when the foe begins a ditch or a wagon ring (`isDigging`), unless its general chose to wait; stakes do not provoke it. Attackers that stand off for 8 rounds (6.4 minutes, `DECLINED`) with nobody coming at them and none of theirs at grips leave the field (run): who sought the battle and will not fight it goes home, so a battle never stands idle to the lull. Only an advancing side enters a ford or a bridge.

### The ambush site

The screen picks an ambush only where a road or a trail leads through the battle's cell (`BattleSite.hasRoad`), and the field of an ambush is laid on that road, at its point nearest the battle point (`findSite`). The column marches on the road nearest the middle of the field, within 0.35 of its height (`ROAD_REACH`). In an ambush given by hand where there is none it goes straight on along the attack, unless that leads it into water, a bog or over a cliff: then by the way round them (`findMarch`, `Battlefields.leadWay`: fords and bogs cost it double, deep water and cliffs bar it), kept by the ground it was found over.

The ambushers choose where the ambush is sprung (`findLairs`): of the stretches of the road the column fits on the field, a front apart, the one whose lairs are worth most, the better side counting in full and the other by half; on even ground the middle. A lair is a line parallel to the road there, 45 to 120 m from the flank of the column (`AMBUSH`), looked at in five spots across the first line of a wing. Its worth: the share of it under trees, doubled (reeds hide half as well); up to half a point for standing 10 m above the road, as much against for standing below it; half a point off for the share of it whose dash to the column goes through a bog or a ford; three off for the share that is barred: off the field, in water, across deep water or a cliff from the column, or nearer than 0.8 of the nearest to any part of it where the road bends. It lies as near as it may, a javelin's throw, under trees or in the open, so the line closes before the column finds its feet. Measured on 2026-11-17, while ambushers in the open still lay as far as they could, over 64 ambushes on real maps, with the ambushers at 0.72, 0.55 and 0.4 of the column's troops: they won 59, 20 and none, and the column lost 38%, 16% and 5% of its men, fallen and taken. Their riders keep their wing where the whole line is under trees.

### Rehearsal

A tactic given by hand before the start (a `tactic` intervention, `getHandTactic`) wins over both the scores and the rehearsal: that side has no options to rehearse, and the foe rehearses against it. `getTactics` lists what the menu offers, the likeliest first: for a side drawn up on its ground, every attack and defence tactic, the crossing only for defenders behind one and the Wagenburg only for a side that may build (`getDrawUpTactics`), whether its general would pick it or not; the two ambuscades for ambushers; lines and ring for besiegers of a walled burg, who ring it in by hand at any odds. The column, the garrison and the landing waves have no other.

Scores only rank the tactics. Before an open fight (field, naval, air) each general plays every option the scores allow him (`getOptions`) in a battle of his own (`rehearse`): a copy of the setup with no dice and nobody moved by hand, but with the orders and morale shifts given before the start, run to its end or for 360 ticks (48 minutes), the foe using the tactic the scores give him. An attacker weaker than the defenders has one option more: not to attack; on ground with water, walls or cliffs, before works laid in advance that bar somebody or a foe expected in a Wagenburg, or where his blows would land the weaker for the ground (`getPurchase`: a crest), so does one short of 1.4 times their power (`WARY`): strong ground asks for more. A rehearsal sees 48 minutes, so a slow win across a ford can lose to waiting. He takes the tactic with the best outcome: holding the field first, then the difference in losses; of equal ones, the likelier by score. Leaving the field before any blow is struck hand to hand (a pursuit is none) costs nothing but the losses: it is not a lost battle. Both do it against the expected foe, not against what the other settled on: the second to choose would otherwise always win. Where water or walls make ticks dear, or works laid in advance that bar somebody (wagons, stakes before a foe with riders), only such an attacker rehearses: his likeliest tactic against waiting. An attacker with engines before standing wagons or a foe expected in a Wagenburg also plays his likeliest tactic storming the wagons at once rather than battering them first (`side.storms`). Against a foe expected in a Wagenburg the attackers rehearse only their likeliest tactic, and when a Wagenburg is among anybody's options or tactics every rehearsal is played for half the ticks, 180: a ring of wagons costs the riders outside it a way round every round.

The other types have one choice each (`getOptions`): ambushers lie on both sides of the road or on one (`ambuscadeOneSide`), only on one where trees hide them on one side alone or only one side has a lair that fits (`findLairs`), and with shooters fall on the column at once or shoot from cover first (`side.waits`, see the ambush phase); defenders ready for a landing or a bombardment (`isFromSea`) meet it at the water's edge or wait inland (`side.waits`); besiegers with two thirds of the odds the walls ask for storm at once (`side.storms`) or keep to the rule; a burg they cut off they starve out instead. A storm is played for half the ticks, 180 (24 minutes) and only with 28 battalions or fewer on the field: more cannot be reckoned within the second.

A rehearsal is the real engine on a copy of the battle with `rehearsal` set and a copy of the field, so a storm in the mind breaches no walls; the regiments are shared. A game drawn up alike, the same tactic and choices on both sides, is played once: an option of one side is often what the other expects of it. Changes of the armies or the type before the start call `rethink`. Planning times are with the ways, under The battalion.

### Phase

The phase is the state the general puts his side in. The screen does not show it: the user sees and gives [orders](#the-sides-order). It is decided from the appraisal with fixed thresholds and never rolled:

- **stand off** (skirmish, shelling, maneuvering): close to shooting distance and no further. The formation halts two fronts from the foe at the nearest, out of reach of its line (`plan.halt`); an army made for shooting (`grip <= fire`) stands there as long as it likes, unless its shots fall short of that halt (`plan.reach`, big battalions) and it has not fired: then it closes as the others do. A general with the better fire keeps it up for 4 to 12 rounds (3 to 10 minutes), by his share of the fire; one with the worse fire closes once the first blow is struck when attacking, unless made for shooting, and one with nothing to shoot with closes from the start, not waiting for the approach to end; when defending he holds his ground unless he is the stronger. A side with engines that comes at a foe behind standing wagons **batters** them first (`getFort`): its formation comes on at the pace of its engines and halts once each has a wagon in range, never nearer than two fronts to the foe, and it stands off, skirmishes or not, till its engines have knocked two stretches down (`BREACHES`); then it closes and goes in by the gaps. It closes before only when the foe comes out of the wagons, or when its general chose to storm them at once. Engines that would not reach the wagons from their row behind a line halted two fronts out (range × 0.9 less how far behind the first line they stand, under two fronts: battalions of 1000 men and more) batter nothing, and the side closes at once; they knock down what comes in their range on the way.
- **close** (melee, boarding, dogfight): the formation advances into contact; a defender takes this phase when the foe's line comes within 1.5 fronts.
- **chase** (pursue, chase): the foe retreats, feigns it, or has a quarter of its battalions left in order.
- **run** (retreat, withdrawal, flee): an advancing side calls the attack off once it has lost 15% of its troops fallen, three times the foe's (`REPULSED`; prisoners are not reckoned); or the day is lost (`isLost`): odds under 1:3, under 2:3 with a quarter of the troops gone, under 1:1 with half of them gone, or morale under 25. In a field battle the odds are reckoned on the ground (`getPurchase`): each side's power by how its blows would land on the foe where the foe stands, as the blows reckon it: the striker's footing just before the foe's line over the foe's own, and the foe's wagons between. So a line on a flat top above a slope, behind a ford or a bog or in a wagon ring holds on against odds that would send it off in the open; a uniform slope favours neither. It is final. A general who sees the day lost before it begins takes the strategic retreat (`isHopeless`): read at the end of the draw, the troops standing, their works laid and the morale shifts given by hand counted, as the first round will read it; the side is drawn up anew in it, runs from the first round with no `broken` entry in the log, and has nothing to rehearse (`getOptions`). A tactic given by hand keeps it on the field for the battle (`plan.defiant`): the odds its general saw do not send it off, only a quarter of its troops lost, morale under 25 or none left in order; an order other than retreat keeps it there while the order stands.
- **feint** (`FEINT`, field battles): a side drawn up in a feigned retreat falls back as if broken once its attack has stalled, once a battle (`plan.lure`): four rounds or more after the first blow (`FEINT_AFTER`), with a battalion at grips, having lost at least as many men as it took (`getFallen`), and with its morale still 70 or more (`WAVERING`), fresh enough to turn. An attack that goes its way never feints: it is a frontal assault. It falls back: its formation four fronts back (`FEINT_BACK`), every battalion running to its place there and standing (`feign`, mission `feint`). Its order shows retreat, to the foe too, who gives chase out of his ground; it is not beaten (`isBeaten`), so the battle does not end on it. It turns (`turnAbout`, logged `turned`) to close once the foe has come two fronts out after it (`LURED`), stood his ground for a round after all, it is back at its places, or after four rounds (`FEINT_ROUNDS`); the pursuers' battalions in order, shooters and engines apart, lose 15 morale (`DUPED`, by their steadiness). A side that loses heart on the way, morale under 30 or the day lost, runs for good. An order to attack turns it as well, to retreat makes the retreat real.
- **surrender** (`SURRENDER`, of every type): a side lays down its arms as a body only by an order by hand, or a garrison that gives its burg up (see the siege). Its battalions in order lay down their arms at once (`capitulate`), and its broken battalions with no way off the field with them. It is final. A side that runs leaves behind those it cannot get away: battalions at grips with no way off the field (`isCornered`) surrender as it turns; the rest break off and run.
- **ambush**: surprise and shock last five rounds (four minutes) of fighting from the first shot or blow (`SURPRISE`); a shocked general gives no orders, and his battalions fend for themselves: they fight who falls on them and shoot back, but go for nobody (`stunned` in `decide`), unless told to by hand. The ambushers' shooters lie in front and shoot from the first tick; the line falls on the column at once, between them. Ambushers who shoot first (`waits`) do not advance: their line keeps where it lies and their shooters shoot, the order shown is skirmish; they go over (`spring`) once the column's morale is under 70, any of them is at grips, the surprise is spent or they have nothing to shoot with. Once the surprise is spent, ambushers the column outweighs by a quarter and more slip away (run) while none of them is at grips and its morale is 70 or more: a raid from cover, not a stand.
- **landing**: the fight for the shore lasts while any battalion of the attackers in order, ships apart, is still on the water (`isAfloat`); the defenders stand ready to meet it.
- **bombardment**: a fleet with no troops to land against troops ashore. Ships keep to the water they sail: the sea, a lake, a river too deep to wade, not a ford or a brook (`shuns`); a fleet cannot join a field without such water (`hasShipWater`), and withdrawn or broken it leaves by water, or at once from water with no way off the field (`isLandlocked`). Ships keep to the water, so nobody comes to grips (ships of a land battle fight only ships hand to hand, `canGrip`): each side has one phase it holds (bombardment, defense) until it runs (withdrawal, retreat). That one phase says nothing of who comes to whom: the fleet sails in and the troops meet it or wait inland as they were drawn up or ordered. The day is lost by fire against fire (`isLost` on `fire` for `power`), and only foes that shoot dishearten a battalion (`getHand`): troops with nothing that reaches the ships leave the shore at once. A landing and a bombardment are fought on the shore nearest the battle point (`BattleSite.getShore`: the water's edge of the sea or of a lake that is not dry, never land cut off by the map's edge; a cell and a half off at most, else the attack is refused) or at the harbour of a port, the axis from the water to the land (`getLandward`).
- **siege**: the besiegers storm at the odds the walls ask for, bombard with engines, else blockade. Engines fire while that brings the walls down before the day is out (`getBatteringRounds`: to the breaches the besiegers' odds ask for, or to the three that still weaken the walls, the engines reckoned at the half strength they keep up tired), and ten rounds longer (`settings.lullRounds`), from the start or after a storm called off, to break the garrison or draw it out; then they hold their fire, the siege is a blockade and the lull ends it. A blockade that loses men to the garrison's engines for ten rounds is raised. The besiegers they call a storm off when it has lost a third of those odds and nobody is on the walls, and go back to their lines. The besieged shelter or bombard, sortie at engines whose guards the party outweighs, come out in force when half as strong again as the besiegers, or the stronger at all under their engines' fire, and surrender when their morale is spent or stormers stand in the burg with none of the garrison in order within it (the burg is taken).

A ring cuts the burg off (`isCutOff`, `battle.cutOff`, logged when it changes) while every gate has a besieger in order no further from it than the ring stands from the walls, and 70 m more (`watches`), so a sortie must clear a stretch of the ring to open a gate: a ship by a quay, troops by a road gate, either by a river gate.

A sortie forms up before it goes (`musterSortie`, `plan.muster`): its party marches out by the gate on the side of its prey (the besiegers' middle for a garrison coming out in force) to rows of up to four abreast a front out of the gate, facing the prey, pushed up to three fronts further out till every place stands on dry ground out of the walls and 1.75 fronts clear of the foe (`MUSTER_CLEAR`); of the gates that have such room the one nearest the prey, and never one on the far side of the burg from it, or the party would form up only to march round the walls. Who comes out first goes furthest, so nobody stands in the way of those behind. It goes for the prey once two thirds of the party stand within half a front of their places, after half as long again as its slowest battalion's march, two to eight rounds (`MUSTER_ROUNDS`), or when a foe comes within 1.5 fronts of it (`launchSortie`); the rounds a sortie at engines may stay out are counted from then. When the sortie is over its party goes back to the places it left (`muster.homes`). With no gate that has room, or a party of one, it goes out at once.

### The side's order

The banner, the log and the marker legend (`battle.course`) show **orders** (`Command`: hold, fortify, skirmish, attack, defend, pursue, retreat, surrender), never phases. `getCommand` puts a phase as an order; `getOrder` shows `fortify` while the side builds (`plan.fortify`), by hand or by its general. In the open (`isOpen`) it also takes `side.advancing`: a side that waits holds where it would skirmish and defends where it would attack.

`COMMANDS` lists the orders a side of each battle type can be given by hand and the phase each takes; any side can be told to surrender, and after the first tick nothing undoes it. `obey` stores the order in `plan.order`, sets `advancing` by it (not in a siege, where the phase does) and changes the phase when the current one does not carry the order out already; the orders are rewritten at once, but the formation moves and the round is counted only at the next round. The order stands for eight minutes, ten rounds (`settings.orderRounds`): meanwhile the general neither changes the phase nor goes over to the attack; then `release` hands the side back to him: in the open he goes over to the attack if he would have (`isAttacking`), and never calls off one under way.

In an ambush, a landing and a bombardment the phases are states of the fight (surprise, shock, landing, prepared defense, bombardment), so an order to hold, skirmish, attack or defend leaves the phase alone (`keepsPhase`): `choosePhase` lets it take its course, short of running or chasing, and the battalions read the order instead of the phase (`getBearing`). Held off (`keepsOff`), ambushers keep their places instead of hunting, wings guard, the frame halts at shooting distance and the reserve is not thrown in; told to skirmish, shooters of a formation that does not move go as far as it takes. A side stood by an order has not begun: the round of the first blow (`battle.engaged`) is not set by nobody marching while an order stands, or a landing held off the shore would be over before it started.

### Orders

Every battalion has a **place** in the formation, across and along its **frame** (a point and a heading), and a **mission**:

| Mission  | Meaning                                                                                                                                          |
| -------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| line     | keep the place; fight what comes to the front                                                                                                    |
| support  | shooters: keep the place behind the line, shoot, stay out of reach                                                                               |
| battery  | engines: stand and shoot                                                                                                                         |
| guard    | keep the place and go only for riders about, the foe pointed at, or what comes right at it: riders on a wing, the weak wing of an oblique attack |
| reserve  | wait behind until sent                                                                                                                           |
| flank    | go to a point, fighting only who bars the way: round the end of the foe's line, over the wall in a storm                                         |
| hunt     | go for the foe worth most anywhere: riders come round, a reserve sent, stormers within the walls, ambushers, pursuers                            |
| withdraw | leave the field                                                                                                                                  |
| feint    | run back to its place in the formation fallen back, as if broken, and stand there                                                                |

The general moves the frame, not the battalions (`moveFrame`): forward at the pace of the slowest battalion of the first line along the way its troops would take, the wings leading an army of riders alone, turning to face the foe, and not at all while most of the line lags behind its places, nor while the side fortifies or holds works and does not advance: places and their stretches stay together. So a river is crossed like an inchworm: the frame waits at the ford until the line is over. The shape of the formation is its timing: the massed wing of an oblique attack strikes first because the other wing stands further back, the wings of an envelopment meet the foe before its centre, a wedge leads with the centre.

A battalion going to its place spreads the way over the ticks left in the round (`keep`), so riders march beside the foot instead of dashing to the next place and waiting there. The screen draws each block trailing the battle by under a tick (`updateBlocks`), which evens out the jostle of a crowd.

**Turned-out flanks** (`hookFlanks`, `plan.hooks`). A side that holds its ground in a field battle, or the defenders of a landing, turns battalions out at an end of its line: a short line beyond the end, from the end battalion's front back, its back against the end battalion's outer side and facing out, clear of the rows behind the line and with room between its battalions to wheel, as strong as the foes it meets there, three battalions at most. Foot of the reserve and of the lines behind turn out as soon as the foe has riders on that wing within 9 fronts of its end (`THREAT`), before they come round: riders further off would swing from one wing to the other as either side's frame turns, and the ends would trade the turned-out battalions back and forth; the first line wheels back from the end in, as far as no foe stands before it within a charge, only for foes level with the line and within a charge of its end. The more threatened end is served first, and an end with no foe at it lends its reserve. Those in a hook keep their places in it, newcomers fall in behind; nobody sends them elsewhere, and they build nothing. Stand, crescent and crossing keep the reserve behind the end battalions for this (`ends`).

The **reserve** is sent, nearest battalion first:

1. into the place of a battalion of the first line that broke with foes near: the second line counts as a reserve for this;
2. against foes come round a flank or through the line, or striking a battalion in the flank or rear, riders first, as many at each as match its strength;
3. at a foe that wavers at grips (morale under 40), to break it;
4. all of it, once the lines have fought for eight rounds (6.5 minutes): a reserve never used is wasted.

Before all that, and in a formation that does not move too, the shooters and engines are guarded (`guardWards`): a foe in order that goes for one hand to hand within 3.4 battalion fronts has the nearest battalion free to go sent at it, of the reserve, the riders on guard and the line troops not at grips, the engines' foes first. Like any battalion sent, it comes back once its foe is gone. Nobody goes out over the side's works for a foe that fights over them.

A battalion sent comes back to its place when its foe is done for.

**The flying wedge** keeps its riders apart from the line in a frame of its own (`plan.wedge`, role `wedge`): a point, then rows of two, three and so on, each behind the gaps of the one before (`formWedge`). It waits two fronts beyond the end of the line, on the wing with the better ride (`chooseLane`: open ground and a fall towards the foe on the way round his end and in to it, and the weaker wing of his), and goes once the ride round the foe takes no less than the walk of the line up to him (`isWedgeDue`), a side that closes from the start too; one that waits for the foe sends it when the lines meet. While it waits its post is kept on the field as a body, like any formation. It goes as a body at the pace of its slowest, waiting for those left behind, round the end of the foe's line to a spot three fronts beyond it, out of reach of the end battalion, and level with its rear (`getVia`: of three, the higher and the more open), then in at the end battalion from beyond it: only once it is level with his line, or as far round as the field lets it, is it at his flank. A wedge held up on the way by a fight, most of its riders off their places, breaks up into it. A wedge held up on the way, most of its riders off their places and some of them at grips, breaks up into that fight there and then. Within a charge of it the wedge waits till the line has that battalion at grips, so it cannot turn to meet the riders, till the lines are at grips without him, or till its own side stops advancing; then it breaks up into a charge (`charging`): every battalion of it hunts, the end battalion pointed at.

A battalion moved by hand keeps the place it was put in.

**Tasks**. After the start the user commands battalions instead of placing them: a `task` change gives the picked battalions of one side a `Task` (`assign`): move to a point, attack a foe in order, pursue a broken one or protect a friend; or, on its own spot, the orders of `getTasks` for its kind: hold, fortify (on foot, where the side may build), skirmish (shooters), defend (who comes to grips, not in a bombardment), retreat (not the besieged) and surrender. Sent to a point, the battalions keep their offsets from their middle and face the way they go. `decide` reads the task before the order, after the fight at grips: moving, a battalion goes for no foe, and once there stands, shooting what comes in range; holding, it does the same on its spot; defending, it also charges a foe within `COUNTER` of the spot, as a line that waits does; fortifying, it builds the stretch before the spot while no foe is near (`getStretchLine` reads the task's spot; never wagons, which are the train's); skirmishing, a shooter comes within range of the foe worth most and steps back from foes that come at it, as `support` does; attacking or pursuing, it charges the foe, or shoots it from within range; protecting, it goes for the foes within two fronts of the friend, else keeps a front beside it. Retreat and surrender stand as no task: the battalion is `leaving` at once, or gives itself up. The general still gives it orders, which it takes up once the task is over (`isTaskOver`), but sends it on no errand of his own meanwhile: not as a reserve, a guard of the shooters, into a gap or a hook: the foe is out of order (for a pursuit: no longer on the run), the friend is, the battalion breaks or leaves, or the task has stood `settings.orderRounds` rounds, as an order to the side does.

## The battalion

Every tick a battalion looks at the foes in sight and at its friends beside it, and chooses, in this order:

1. **Broken or withdrawn**: it runs for the edge of the field behind its side; broken, while a foe in order is near or it was shot at since the last round. A regiment withdrawn by hand once the battle is on (`withdraw`) has its battalions marked `leaving`: they march off whatever the general says, he no longer counts them, and they do not hold the field. Withdrawn before the start, the regiment is dropped with its add, moves and turns, as if it never came.
2. **At grips**: it fights; with nobody to its front it turns on whoever has it by the flank.
3. **Its work**: a builder of a fortifying side at its place builds its stretch (`build`) while no foe in order is within a round's march and a charge of it, see [Works](#works).
4. **Its own skin**: shooters step back from those who come for them within 55 m, unless works keep them off (riders before stakes, anyone outside wagons); riders that shoot do so on any errand, from foes within 55 m of their front, and ride within shot of a foe they would charge; a shaken battalion (morale under 30) does not attack, nor one of a column in shock. Who shoots from its place (shooters, engines, line troops that throw, a battalion holding a spot by hand) shoots a broken foe in range once no foe in order is (`inRange`).
5. **Its order**, by mission. A line battalion goes for a foe within reach of its place and no further, so the line holds: 2.75 battalion fronts when its side advances and closes, one front when it waits. A foe a neighbour holds is within reach half as far again, to be taken in the flank. Behind its own works, on a side that holds, it does not go for a foe the works lie between: it lets it come into contact.
6. **Its target** (`getWorth`): of the foes its mission lets it reach, the nearest by the way there, made nearer by all that makes it a better prey: held by a friend and open to a blow in the flank or rear, shaken, hitting harder than it stands, pointed at by the general; for riders a shooter or an engine, and not formed line troops head on. Friends already going for it make it further, so the line spreads.

The way to a point of the field is kept while the point stays (`getPointKey`), the way to a foe is found anew every round, sought toward it (A\*, `seekWay`); `Pathfinding.findWay` keeps such ways by the content of the grids they were sought over, and `findNearest` the distances and turns to the nearest node by a foe or a point, so a rehearsal asking for one another already found gets it at once; the screen drops them all when it closes (`Pathfinding.clear`). A spread knows the parts of its grid that can be entered once for the grid (`getParts`), so a node it can never come to costs it nothing. Distances to the foes come from one spread from where the battalion stands, kept till it has gone 5 nodes (`KEPT`) and spread only as far as the foes asked about (`costTo`), and `pick` asks about a foe only when its worth over the straight line could beat the best: no way is shorter. A field without water skips the wading test (`isDry`), and what the ground costs outlives the works rising and falling. Stormers all make for the middle of the burg, so their ways are found once from there for each kind of troops (`getSharedHeading`), and besiegers pick their foes as the crow flies: forty stormers each finding their own way on one tick cost 150 ms. Measured on 2026-11-12 over every battle type, three passes: planning up to 331 ms, six ticks up to 64 ms, one tick up to 51 ms.

A battalion about to strike a foe runs in at three times its pace (`CHARGE_HASTE`) once the foe is within what it covers in one tick so: about 29 m on foot, 84 m on horse. Engines never charge, the broken run at 1.2 times their pace (`FLIGHT`). A step checks its whole way, a sample every 11 m, under the contact gap: it ends short of the first battalion it would touch, so a charge ends at contact, and it refuses water and shunned ground anywhere on the way, not only at its end. A battalion in order never steps or is pushed over the edge of the field, only those on the run leave it; one caught on a ford or a bridge when its side stops advancing goes off it, but never into the deep (`isDeep`). Pace is read where the step starts.

Battalions do not pass through each other, except friends packed within the walls of one burg, who are not in the way of one stepping in or out either: a garrison once jammed itself in its own gate. A stretch of wall (not a gate) keeps bodies apart: who stands on either side of it is not in the other's way and is not pushed. Prisoners stand aside: nobody is held up by them. One hemmed in by friends goes round them by the side it took first (`block.detour`), which is why riders kept back stand behind the ends of the line.

**Giving way** (`noteTraffic`, `giveWay`). A battalion that came less than a quarter of its leg, with its next leg open ground (no water, no wall), is _held up_ from that tick on, and asks the first friend it would walk into straight on to give way. The friend gives way if it stands idle; if both make for the same opening (next turns within a front of each other), the one further from it gives way; otherwise unless it goes the same way (within 60°: it is ahead in the same queue) or is itself held up longer (ties by id); and never while it fights, shoots or builds. Giving way is a step off the asker's line on its own side or on ahead of it, never back at it, or standing when it has no room, and it lasts till the asker is by it, a round at most (`Yielding`): it does not step back into the way next tick. So a gate, a bridge, a ford or a breach is passed in the order battalions come to it, idle friends step out of a column's way, faster troops overtake slower ones, and two coming at each other never wait for each other. Within a front of walls a battalion held up turns at most a radian off its way (`ASKEW`), and nowhere does a step turned further off go through a gate: it waits its turn rather than wander about the streets or walk back in. A battalion held up by friends for two rounds (`PATIENCE`, 96 s) files through them all the same, neither in their way nor pushed by them, till it stands clear, whether it moves that tick or fights: no jam outlasts it. The traffic is kept a tick at a time and is nothing after a replay from the start.

**Ways by walls**. A way starts and ends at the grid node of the point, or, by a wall or works, at the nearest node on the point's side of them (`getNodeAt`): a battalion a metre outside the wall stands in a node whose middle is within it, and was led straight into the wall every tick. Troops on the run go for the node about them nearest the edge that no wall or works bar them from (`getFlight`), so the broken get out of a burg by its gates. Stretches of wall and of cliff are indexed by the grid nodes their bounds take in (`getWallsBetween`, `getCliffsBetween`): a short way is checked against the stretches about it only.

## Blows and losses

A battalion strikes or shoots with `troops × attack × √upkeep × fortune`, and stands with `troops × defense × √upkeep` (`Military.getBattleUpkeep`): fortune tells on blows only. There is no table of strengths by unit type and phase: what a unit is good at is in its attack and defense, and the phase only says what the side does. Its footing (`getFooting`) and its energy (see [Energy](#energy)) scale both. Footing is what the ground leaves of it: 0.75 in a marsh, half in shallows or a ford and a quarter in deep water for those not afloat, half for a stormer on a rampart, and `1 − grade` on a slope, half at the least, for both sides alike (`getGrade`, the steepest fall where it stands; fliers and ships afloat excepted). A few states change it outright: troops on the run (`isRunning`) strike at a tenth (`BROKEN`) and stand as well as ever, a side in shock strikes and stands at half (`SHOCK`). Walls are one factor against blows and shots alike: troops within them lose a quarter of the men they would to foes outside (`WALLS`); woods and houses let 60% of the shots tell (`COVER`). A side whose phase is idle (`isIdle`: blockade, sheltering, waiting) holds its fire.

Men lost a tick are the blows that land on a battalion over what each of its men stands with: `Σ strike × impetus × cover × rate ÷ (defense per man) ÷ 6`, carried in fractions, so they come to the same men every time. A battalion loses whole men, or ships; when the battle is over the parts its battalions lost are counted together by regiment and unit (`settleLosses`), so a fleet of two-ship battalions that each lost a third of a ship has lost a ship. The rate is a round's kills per point of attack against defense 1, the same for a blow and a shot: 0.0125 (`KILL_RATE`), so what a unit shoots with is in its attack alone. Losses grow with the blow, with no ceiling, so numbers and concentration tell. A blow or a shot lands 25% harder in the flank and 80% in the rear (`FLANK`, `REAR`), by the bearing from the target's facing, and shakes the troops hit the more. Works of the target's side between the two take their share too, see [Works](#works). **Impetus** multiplies the strike: a blow's is set when contact begins (none over works), `1 + closing speed in m/s ÷ 6.2` from the striker's own velocity towards its target (`block.vx`, `block.vy`, set by `step`), at most 5 (`MAX_IMPETUS`): 1.58 for foot at the charge, 2.69 for horse at the gallop, 1.19 for foot that walks into a foe, and loses 15% of its excess every tick at grips; a shot's is `1.5 − distance ÷ range`, 0.5 to 1.5.

## Works

A side can build **works**: lines on the field before its first line that hinder its foes and shelter it (`FieldWork` in `battlefield.ts`, the rules in `works.ts`). One kind of line, three kinds:

|                             | stakes                               | ditch                                                          | wagons                                                       |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| a stretch is built in       | 15 ticks (2 min) by a full battalion | 60 ticks (8 min) by a full battalion                           | 10 ticks (80 s) by the side's train, three stretches at once |
| blows and shots over it     | × 1                                  | × 1                                                            | × 0.5                                                        |
| impetus of a charge over it | none                                 | none                                                           | none                                                         |
| crossing it                 | riders never                         | everybody, at a ford's pace while astride it, its own side too | nobody, until a stretch is knocked down                      |
| contact over it             | not for riders                       | yes                                                            | not for riders                                               |

Only wagons shelter: stakes stop riders, a ditch slows everybody, neither takes anything off a blow or a shot. A block is astride a ditch while its length the way it heads crosses one, of either side, and goes at a ford's pace (`isInDitch` in `getStride`). Riders neither cross wagons nor fight over them: wagons are the answer to horse and bows. Foot fight the holders over them, and only engines open a way in.

**The test.** A work counts when it stands (finished by the start of this round and not knocked down), is held by the side of the block it shelters (the target of a blow or a shot, the foe of a block that would cross it), and the straight line from one block's middle to the other's crosses it (`getWorksBetween`). No facing is needed: a foe that came round the end has no work between. A side's own works never hinder it, but for a ditch: that slows everybody. Grid nodes are not marked: a 6 m strip is finer than the grid.

**A stretch** runs across the front of its battalion's place, as long as the block and the line's gap, so neighbours' stretches join, 6 m ahead of its front: at grips it lies between the two. Its front is on the left of `a` → `b`. Places of rank 0 get stretches, line troops or shooters on foot, whoever stands there; riders, engines, ships and fliers never build. A stretch is found by the place, so one left behind by a formation that moved on is not its battalion's any more. A wagon line is the train's: a stretch before each battalion of the first line, joined end to end where the line does not stand level (a crescent, a brow): a step of up to 6 m is taken up by the next stretch, a larger one gets a stretch of its own across it, so no gap opens. At each end two more turned back to the rear, 45° and 90°, their fronts out, to a half circle (none in the water or off the field; none in a siege, whose lines ring a burg in). Against a foe whose riders make a third of its power the line is closed behind the side (`closeWagons`): from the ends of the half circle straight back past its rearmost place, then across, pulled in where that would run off the field; no stretch is laid in water, which closes the ring there. Shooters stay in front of wagons; riders would ride round them into the side's back. A wagon ring is a closed polygon round a Wagenburg, a stretch for each battalion on the ring, square to it at its own radius, out past the corners of its front, with a short step where neighbours' radii differ and stretches more across a gap wider than 120°.

**Taking effect.** Stretches finished in a round stand from the start of the next, all at once (`raiseWorks`, logged `fortified` when the last one the side still works on stands: a stretch left behind half built does not count, nor for the foe's reaction to digging). When one of them bars somebody the ways are forgotten, at most once a round. The ways are rough for a block only when something bars it: water or walls, the foe's wagons, the foe's stakes for riders; a ditch never makes a field rough. Barring goes through `isBarred` (steps, the closed steps of the ways, the push-through check, the pushes that keep battalions apart), and a step stops with the block's front half a metre short of a work that bars it. `inContact` refuses contact over works that bar the rider of the two.

**Building.** A battalion builds `troops / battalion / ticks` of its stretch a tick and keeps the kind it began: wagons when the foe's riders and shooters make a third of its power and the train has the front of the line done before the foe comes (the half circle; its back is closed last), a ditch when the foe's riders are under a third and it is done in time, else stakes. A battalion that begins wagons has the train lay the whole line; the train builds the side's wagons while it fortifies, three stretches at a time, those begun first (`AT_ONCE`). A battalion of a fortifying side at its place builds while no foe in order is within the foe's round of march and a charge (`pace × 6 + 2.75 fronts`); otherwise it decides as ever, and its work is kept. The general decides once a round (`plan.fortify` in `direct`): a side begins when it holds (neither advances, chases nor runs; besiegers in blockade or bombardment), in the open its general did not choose to wait rather than fight (`side.waits`: else both sides dig till the lull; in a landing it is the choice to stand inland), may build (both sides of a field battle, the besiegers of a siege, the defenders of a landing), something of its works is left, and the foe will not be within a charge before some kind is done. It stops when the works stand, when it advances, or when a foe in order could be upon a battalion of its first line within a round (`isFoeUpon`): then no battalion of the line would build, and the order gives way to the phase's. A Wagenburg keeps building its wagons whatever comes. The foe's arrival (`plan.arrival`) is the gap beyond a charge over how fast it closed last round; in the first round an advancing foe closes at the pace of its first line; a foe not closing never comes, one within a charge is there. Once begun a side goes on until its works stand or it advances, so the order does not flicker; a Wagenburg fortifies from the first tick. `Fortify` by hand stands its time like any order (in a landing it keeps the phase); another order by hand ends it. While a side builds with a foe in order within the field's width, the lull is held off.

**Dug in.** A side dug in starts with the works its rule would build with all the time there is, finished, laid at its places after the moves by hand and anew whenever the troops are drawn up (`digIn`). It is the `dig` intervention, set before the start; by default the besiegers ringing a burg in (works along the ring, facing the walls) and the defenders of a landing holding the water's edge. Nobody builds a stretch that would lie in the water (`isAtSea`).

**Holding works.** A side with standing works before its first line holds them: it does not go over to the attack because the foe does not come, but does when the foe is spent or outshoots it. A Wagenburg goes out by sallies only: its reserve is sent once its side advances.

**Engines** of a side that fights on knock the foe's wagons down, each a wagon of the stretch nearest it within its range, one a round (`breakWagons`): `GAP_WAGONS` (2) of them open a gap, about a grid node wide, that troops get in by. Its `harm` is then 1, the stretch counts no more, kept for the drawing, logged `gap`. Ditches and stakes are not knocked down. A side with engines therefore batters wagons before it goes in, see [Phase](#phase).

On screen works are a layer of their own (`#battleWorks`) between the ground and the fire, redrawn when their signature changes and recorded by the animation export. Hover lines with a tooltip per stretch lie in `#battleWorkTips`, which the export leaves out.

## Ground

A field unit is a metre. The field is the map at true scale, sized by its battalions (`getFieldSize`): a full battalion stands a metre a man across five ranks, so 60 m for 300 men, 40 m for 200, 200 m for 1000; a bigger battalion stands deeper on the same 200 m (`WIDEST`). The field is 22.7 fronts wide and 0.6 as deep (1364 × 818 m for 300-man battalions), 250 m wide at least and 4.5 km at most, a siege 1.5 times that. A battalion is 0.3, 0.5 or 1 population point, the least that keeps all the troops within `settings.maxBattalions`, 0.2 when 0.3 makes fewer than `minBattalions`; a host too big for 1 point gets as many whole points as keep it within the limit (`getBattalionSize`), so any number of men can be fought out. A burg is at true size too, 100 m² a dweller (radius `10 × √(people / π)`, 1.6 km across for 20,000 people), walled as a medieval town (`getOutline`): its map cell's corners for the plan, evened out about the burg and none sharper than 110° (a tower is set across it), each side a run of straight stretches from tower to tower, at most 0.3 of the radius, bowing out a little and bending with the ground the same way for the same burg; its farthest point at the radius. On the sea or a lake the walls keep to the shore as drawn, and a port's middle stays on its land (`getHarbour`), so a port has a quay side. A siege takes in the whole burg (`getSiegeRadius`) and the land about it, top to bottom: 340 m and a battalion front beyond the walls, room for the ring and behind it; the field is no smaller than its battalions ask for, and they keep their real size. It is at most 1.5 times as wide as its battalions or its ring alone ask for (`SIEGE_ZOOM`), so battalions stay visible: a burg too big for that is drawn smaller to fit (`fitSiegeBurg`), its port moved onto the shore by the radius drawn. The grid is always 100 × 60 nodes, so a node is 1% of the width, 9 to 45 m, and pathfinding costs the same at every size. The field carries its size: `width`, `height`, `cell` and `front`. The screen hands `prepare` an unbuilt field of the map (`createMapField`), and `prepare` builds it (`build`), again whenever the battalion size changes before the start; an empty field (a naval or an air battle, a test) is resized and its walls raised anew.

Every length in the battle is one of three kinds, named or commented as such:

1. **Physical**, fixed metres at any size: ranges, the long-shot threshold (250 m), point blank, the contact gap (12 m), shooters stepping back (55 m) and their leash (80 m), the default bowshot (205 m) and the siege ring (at most 340 m), ambush distances from the column (45 to 120 m), the sortie's reach (400 m), rivers, fords, bridges, rampart thickness, burg radius, the charge run-in.
2. **Relative to the battalion front**: block width (0.41 to 1.32 fronts by its men) and depth (half a front), gaps in and between lines, the bows and refused wings of formations (`TACTICS`), the bend of a line to the ground and the water's edge it holds, clearance (0.11 fronts; between foes never above 0.9 of the contact gap, or battalions of 500 men and more could never come to grips), the "arrived" tolerance, how far a battalion leaves its place for a foe, guard, ward and morale radii, crowding, the generals' probe offsets, gate and breach widths (a gate is drawn half a front wide and taken 1.2 fronts wide, `GATEWAY`: the stretches of the gateway beside the gate as drawn are its `jamb`s, wall on the plan; a breach 0.7 of a front; two grid nodes at least, so the ways go through them; `getOpening`). The generals reckon with the gates as drawn (`getGates`).
3. **Relative to the field**: the shallows (the nodes of sea or lake next to land, 14 m at the default size), the edge margin (2%), the ellipse the generals reach to, base depths and ground candidates, and on screen the size of marks and lines (`getPoint`: sized as on a field 1000 wide).

A few rules compare a distance with a pace, and read the block's own metres a tick: on the march is who made 1.4 paces in a round, a side closes when the gap shrank by 0.7 of a foot pace, a formation advances a pace a tick a round.

The default 300-man battalion is where the battle was balanced: at that size every length is what it was on the old 1000-wide plan, and the battle plays as it did. Elsewhere physical lengths differ in fronts: a bowshot of 160 m is 4 fronts in a battle of 200-man battalions, 2.7 at the default and 0.8 at 1000 men, so small battles favour who stands and shoots, and large ones take long to close (a 1000-man battle runs 50 to 65 minutes).

The field lies where the battle is named for (`findSite`): on the burg of its cell for a siege; a battle fought before a burg that is no siege, landing or bombardment keeps the burg where it stands (a port on its shore) and moves the field's middle off it, so the burg lies 0.4 of the reach from the middle (`getOutskirts`, `BURG_END`), where the defenders form up, the end before it on dry land, which end by the place; else by the river, on the crossing of a road over it if there is one; an ambush on the road of its cell. What the map draws is taken as drawn: rivers on the Catmull-Rom curves the map draws them with, roads from where those curves bring them onto the scene to where they leave it, land and water by the coastline shapes (a node takes the innermost outline about it, so an island in a lake is land), heights, tree cover and wetness interpolated within the triangle of the three cell centres round each node; a corner beyond the map's edge lends the values of the cell nearest it. The map is coarser than the plan, a cell some tens of km across, so what it cannot show is a fixed pattern of the place: hills by the roughness of the cell (a tenth of its rise to its neighbours, 4 to 100 m) in two octaves of 520 and 230 m, copses and bogs by the share of the biomes, each node's biome (`field.biomes`) one of the three cells' land biomes by its share, picked by 160 m noise so the border between two biomes runs ragged (a field sited on the sea takes the biome most of its land has), the lake each node lies in by the lake's kind (`field.lake`: 1 + its index in `LAKE_SUBTYPES`); a dry lake is a flat bare bed of the land, troops cross it, and meanders some twelve river widths long. A river is as wide as the flux of the river cell nearest the middle of the field gives it, `12 + ⅔√flux` m from 16 to 52: a brook of 18 m or less (flux 81) is waded anywhere, a river of 26 m or less (flux 441) at fords 72 m long every 480 to 880 m, a wider one only by a bridge. Measured on 2026-11-18 over 12 default maps: 11%, 8.7% and 1.2% of land cells, some 11 great rivers a map.

**Roads** go as the ground lets them (`leadRoad`, `leadWay`): the map's line is straight at this scale, so between the points it comes onto the scene and leaves it, by the burgs it passes and the ends of roads that join it, a road takes the cheapest way over the grid, in steps to the 16 nodes about a node. A stretch costs its length, and over again for a climb of 0.08 (`EASY`), by the square of the steepness; twice more through a bog, eight times over a river, so it bridges one once; up to once more for ground too fine to show (two noises of 310 and 130 m), so it winds a little on a plain; and once more four fronts off the map's line. The sea and lakes bar it. The way is evened out to a curve, never into water. Where no way is found the road keeps the map's line. A road crosses a river square to the stream (`squareCrossings`): the stretch from 14 m and a node beyond one bank to as far beyond the other is laid straight across the stream where the road met it, and the road within twice that of the crossing is dropped, so it comes to the bridge anew; left as it was where a bank has no dry room for that or the road would cross again. Bridges, gates and road nodes follow the road as led. A route is cut to the view by its curve, not by the straight lines between its points (`bowsIn`): a field off the cell's middle keeps its road.

The field's heights are a shape, `elevation` 0 to 1, times `relief`: the rise from the lowest node to the highest, in metres. `base` is the lowest node's height above sea level, so the drawn heights are true ones. The relief (`BattleReliefView.getMarkup`) is drawn from a cubic B-spline over the nodes, sea and lakes filled from the shore, in one of two styles: hachures, strokes from each level down the fall line, or hairline contours fringed with short ticks on their downhill side. Both take the finest round interval that draws no more than 12 lines (24 rows of hachures) over the rise, so a field a few metres high gets lines a foot apart. Every fifth line is an index line; the index lines are labelled where two of them cross the field, every line where fewer do. Tops (▲) and hollows (▽) are marked with their heights by prominence: how far a top rises above the lowest pass to higher ground, or a hollow sinks below its lowest outlet, the field's edge counting as either; a tenth of the field's rise and 0.3 m at least. Hollows by water are the shore, not marked. A soft shadow from the north-west is optional. Hachures and shadow grow with the field's rise, full at 80 m, so a low field draws them faint; the least slope that draws a hachure and the shadow's exaggeration follow the field's own steep slope, the one only a twentieth of it exceeds, so a gentle field still shows its form. `getElevation` interpolates it between the nodes and `getSlope` reads its rise over run along a heading. Pace on a slope follows Tobler's hiking function cubed by the way a battalion goes (`getClimb`), fliers and ships excepted: 1.7 times the pace 3° downhill, 0.69 of it 2° uphill, a third 6° uphill, a tenth from 12° uphill and 18° downhill; the ways round water and walls do not weigh slopes, but weigh mud, and are found anew when it sets in. The walls and towers of a walled burg count as 15 m of height for whoever stands within them, except in a breach (`WALL_HEIGHT`): defenders shoot further out and besiegers shorter in, and climbing onto them or down from them is as slow as such a slope. Only who climbs them (`isClimbing`) feels that height, and only over a stretch of them: a gate and the streets within are level, or every gate would be a 29° climb at a seventh of the pace. Range is as far as a missile flies at the best angle, `range × √(1 + 2h ÷ range)` for a shooter `h` m above its target (`getRange`): archers of 160 m reach 196 m from 40 m above and 113 m from 40 m below, none at all from half their range below; the shot's force, read from that range, is the less uphill. Nobody shoots a foe nearer than point blank: 2 m for bows, 20 m for engines (`canShoot`).

**Cliffs** (`FieldCliff`, `field.cliffs`) are stretches nobody on foot or horse crosses, their top on the left of `a` → `b`, `height` metres from foot to top. They bar like a wall with no gate (`isCliffed` in `isBarred`), so steps, ways, flight and surrender (`hasWayOut`) go round them. Nobody fights hand to hand across one unless one of the two flies (`inContact`), and nobody stands in the way of a battalion on the other side of one; shots go over. A field with cliffs is rough (`isRoughField`), as one with water or walls. Cliffs drawn on a hand-made field scale with it when its battalions size it.

Cliffs are laid in `cliffs.ts` on the grid alone (`CliffGround`), by marching squares through the middles of the sides between nodes (`traceEdges`), the stretches of one edge kept end to end in pieces; a piece shorter than two fronts is none, and one that ends at the edge of the field runs on past it, so nobody slips round its end. **Sea cliffs** (`laySeaCliffs`): on a field whose roughness is 30 m or more the coast is cliff where a beach noise (300 m) is over 0.5, never within a front of a road, a river or a burg with its piers (`getClearance`: metres from the road and river nodes, spread over the grid, and from each burg's edge), nor within 1.5 fronts of the shore point nearest the middle, so there is always a beach, and no piece runs over six fronts without a beach of 1.5 fronts. They have no step: water reads as the lowest land, so a sea cliff lends no height to shots. **Escarpments** (`raiseEscarpments`), on fields of 50 m roughness or more (a cell 500 m above or below a neighbour: mountain country), not in a siege, stand where the ground already falls: along the line at half the land's rise, a stretch is a cliff where the ground falls across it at 0.12 or more (6.8°), a gap noise of two octaves (420 and 170 m) is over 0.4, and no corner of its square is water or within a front of a road, a river or a burg. Its pieces are cut to six fronts at most with gaps of 1.5 fronts, a piece shorter than two fronts is none, and of two pieces within six fronts of each other the shorter goes (`thin`): the ground about a cliff is its own. A gully, two fronts across, is cut where the hillside is gentlest on the edge of ground the cliffs close off from ground it was one with, until every piece of ground troops on foot reach without cliffs is one with them (`findGully`; at most 24 rounds, else the field has no escarpments). The ground about a cliff (`raiseGround`) is raised on its top side and lowered at its foot by half its height (half the roughness, 6 to 40 m), fading over three fronts from it and over a front toward its ends, the ends measured straight along its first and last stretches; the step (`scarp`) is smooth over the field, and a node's side of the cliff (`top`) is read at its nearest point, at a joint by both stretches by it. So the ground jumps across a cliff and nowhere else: past a cliff's end its side flips where no step is left. `elevation` holds the raised and lowered ground, so the relief, tops and the generals' scores see it; `getElevation` interpolates the ground without the step and adds the step where the point's node is on the top side, sharp at the cliff; `getSlope` reads the ground on the point's own side of a cliff, so the top of one is level ground for footing and pace. A field without cliffs reads its heights exactly as before.

The general counts a cliff between the end of a line and the ground beyond it as cover (`getEnds`) and scores a line across a cliff 2 lower for each stretch of it that one crosses. Defenders ready for a landing on a cliffed shore hold the beach nearest it (`findBeach`).

On a field with cliffs the formation cannot always go where its battalions can: the way round may run where it does not fit, by the field's edge or through a gully. So there:

- **who comes on** is read by the way round (`plan.walk`, `getWalkGap`: one spread from the foes a round, for both sides), not by the gap between the lines: a side walking round a cliff to the foe closes, and the battle does not die down;
- **a formation goes no further than its next turn** (`moveFrame`): past the corner of a cliff the way would lead back, and it would swing to and fro;
- **an advancing side that comes no nearer for four rounds goes in** (`STALLED`): from a stand-off it closes, and once it has closed, every battalion it has, engines too, is sent to hunt by its own way (`sent`, mission `hunt`);
- **a battalion by the corner of a cliff goes round it by its own node** (`getWayNodes`, `getSharedHeading`): the way from the next node on would cut the corner.

The cliffs' steps are shut once a field for all but fliers (`getCliffSteps`), and a way is checked against the stretches of the nodes it passes through (`forNodesAlong`, walls too), so a long way costs no more than a short one. Cliffs are drawn in the relief's ink, a thin line along the top averaged out of the grid's steps, with small teeth down the face, the longer the higher the cliff (`BattleCliffsView.getMarkup`). The relief is drawn from the same sharp ground: its slopes are read on each sample's own side of the step, and contours, their ticks and labels and hachures leave out a band of 0.8 of a node either side of a cliff, so they run into it instead of piling up along it, and the field's steep slope that scales hachures and shade leaves the band out; the diagnostics draw the stretches with a tick on the top side and name high ground and cliffs under the pointer. `CLIFFS.on` switches them off for tests. Measured on 2026-11-15. On generated maps: cliffs on 15 to 22% of land fields, a median of 350 to 470 m of cliff on a field that has any, one to three pieces; no jump of the ground over 1.5 m but across a cliff; building a field a few milliseconds dearer with cliffs, planning under 50 ms and ticks under 0.6 ms on cliffed fields. On 40 hand-made hill fields with cliffs against the same hills without (five patterns, four directions, two army sizes, no ships): battles ran a third longer, the attackers winning 13 of them (9 without), the defenders 27 (30), none a stand-off (one without). See [the PRD](../prd/battle-cliffs.md).

Engines in range of a besieged walled burg, bombarding or covering a storm, work a breach into the wall nearest the strongest of them by the weight of their blows: what would cut down 125 men of defense 1 opens one (`BREACH`, `batter`), three at most (`STORM_ODDS`): a fourth makes the walls no weaker. Walls have gates where roads and rivers (not brooks) come in, and a burg on the sea or a lake sea gates on its quay (`getQuays`): one for every 400 m of its true radius, three at most, on the stretches looking out on the most water and half its drawn radius apart (`FieldWall.water`: 0 a road, 1 the quay, 2 a river). A stone pier goes from each, square to the wall, over the strand and a quarter of the burg's drawn radius out into the water, 300 m at most and never too short to see; a port without walls has its piers all the same. Ships on a land field keep to the water, on the run too (`isMoored`); drawn up on land they put out to the nearest water.

The attackers of a landing are shipped over the sea or a lake at the pace of the ships, and come out of the boats in the **shallows**, the water next to land (`Terrain.shallow`): there they wade at a ford's pace and fight and stand at half, as in a river. Nobody fights hand to hand from a boat (`isAboard` in `inContact`), and the defenders let the boats come (`decide`): their prey is who wades out of them.

## Time

A tick is eight seconds (`TICK_SECONDS`) and a round six ticks, 48 s; rates per tick and per round (losses, morale, impetus decay, breaches, turning) and counts of rounds keep the values they were balanced at. Unit speed is stored in km/h and turned into metres a tick (`toMetresPerTick`): foot 4.3 km/h is 9.6 m a tick. A battle ends after ten rounds without losses (`settings.lullRounds`), counted from the first blow, and not while an advancing side closes the gap (`plan.gap`) or brings its middle nearer the foe's (`plan.apart`: its body marching up behind riders that met first); 600 rounds, eight hours (`settings.maxRounds`), is only a backstop, so a run to the end (a replay, an export) always ends, a battle that ends with nobody driven off is named no more than a plain victory by its losses (`getStatus`), and an order by hand stands eight minutes. At the default size a battle takes 18 to 25 minutes: an approach of a few minutes, then 12 to 17 of fighting. The loser loses a third to two fifths of his army, half of it taken prisoner, the winner 6 to 12%. A tick plays in 160 ms at ×1, fifty times as fast as the battle.

**Time of day.** A battle starts at a whole hour, 0 to 23 (`battle.hour`), drawn from its seed (`getHour`), by day four times in five (`BY_DAY`), the night hours sharing the rest, and set by hand before the start; the log file keeps it. The clock and every log entry show the time of day (`formatClock`, hh:mm:ss), not the time since the start. The light (`getDaylight`) is full by day, 05:00 to 20:00, fades through twilight to 23:00, is none at night and grows again through the twilight from 03:00. The log names the period (`getPeriod`: night, dawn 03–05, morning, midday 11–14, afternoon, evening 17–20, dusk 20–23) at the start and every time the battle lasts into the next (`noteTime`). The sun (`getSun`) rises at 05:00 in the east, stands south at 12:30 north of the equator and north of it south of the equator, as high as at the equinox (90° less the latitude of the place, `battle.latitude`, 15° at least), and sets at 20:00 in the west. Through the light it gives, the hour shortens [sight](#sight).

## Weather

A battle has a `weather` (clear, rain, downpour, snow, fog) and three conditions apart from it (`CONDITIONS`), each on or off and with any sky: `mud`, soaked ground, `ice`, frozen ground and rivers, and `heat`. Mud can follow rain under a clear sky, and rain can fall on dry ground. All are set at the setup and changed at any moment by an intervention (`weather`, `condition`), logged and replayed like a die; before the start a change sets the generals thinking again. The screen sets a battle up with what the climate of its cell is likely to give (`getClimate`): rain the likelier the wetter the cell, a third of it a downpour, snow for it in frost, fog in cool damp places, mud the likelier the wetter and when it rains but never in frost, ice the harder the frost (nine in ten at -11° and colder), heat from 22° on, the likelier the hotter; the same cell always gives the same day.

Effects, as shares of what a battalion has in clear weather on dry ground:

|          | Sight  | Force of shots | Pace on land                                          | Footing | Energy spent      |
| -------- | ------ | -------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- | ------- | ----------------- |
| Rain     | 1500 m | 0.6            | –                                                     | –       | –                 |
| Downpour | 400 m  | 0.35           | –                                                     | –       | –                 |
| Snow     | 600 m  | 0.8            | –                                                     | –       | –                 |
| Fog      | 150 m  | –              | –                                                     | –       | –                 |
| Mud      | –      | –              | 0.5 in the open, 0.75 in woods and bogs, 0.8 on roads | 0.75    | –                 |
| Ice      | –      | –              | 0.75; rivers crossed anywhere, as at a ford           | –       | –                 |
| Heat     | –      | –              | –                                                     | –       | ×2, rest excepted |

- **Sky** (`SKIES`): the murk shortens sight, and with it every range (see [Sight](#sight)); wet strings and powder weaken the force of a shot (`fight`), for every shooter, engines and ships too. The sky slows nobody.
- **Mud** (`MUD`, `getGoing`, `getFooting`): fliers and ships do not mind it. Riders on open ground keep 0.6 of the foot's pace there (`MUD.riders`): a horse sinks deeper than a man, so mud gives the pursuit no more to the horse than dry ground does. Slower troops close slower, so a charge through mud strikes softer, and take longer, so they tire more.
- **Ice** (`ICE`): fliers do not mind it. Every river node counts as a ford (`isFordable`, `field.frozen`, set from `battle.ice` by `prepare` and by the intervention, the ways found anew): troops cross at a ford's pace (0.4) and footing (0.5), and a side that does not advance holds its bank as at any ford. Lakes and the sea do not freeze, and ships keep their water.
- **Heat** (`HEAT`, `tire`): every deed but rest costs twice its energy.

## Sight

What a battalion sees ([sight.ts](../../src/model/political/battle/sight.ts), `Sight`); the generals see all. By day under a clear sky everybody sees the whole field and nothing below is reckoned (`isLimited`): such a battle is fought exactly as it was before sight. Else a battalion sees a foe in the open as far as the dark and the murk let it (`getSight`): the dark keeps it to 120 m at night (`NIGHT`), and as the light fails through dusk, as `120 ÷ (1 − daylight)²`; the sky to its sight (`SKIES`); both together add their murk, `1 ÷ (1/dark + 1/sky)`: fog at night is 67 m. A foe in woods or houses is seen half as far (`COVER`); ambushers lying in wait, still and dark, till the first blow is struck, half as far again (`STILL`, `isLying`); in the dark every other battalion carries torches (`isLit`) and is seen half as far again as it sees (`TORCH`), but besiegers storming the walls, who steal up dark. Whatever the light, 30 m is always seen (`CLOSE`): who is at grips knows his foe.

- **What a battalion knows** (`knows`): the foes it sees, those any battalion of its side in order saw at the start of the round (word passed along the line), and the foe the general or a task points it at. It goes for, shoots at, shrinks from and pursues only those: a broken foe out of sight gets away. The general's choices are his own and see everything.
- **Range**: no shot reaches further than its target is seen (`getRange`): an archer in fog shoots 150 m whatever his bow. The generals plan with the same reach (`getReach`): shooters halt where they reach a foe line at its torches, engines where they reach a burg (lit) or wagons, besiegers ring a garrison out of its reach.
- **Charge**: the last dash at a run is made only at a foe in sight (`run`).
- **Morale**: a loss shakes friends and heartens foes only where they see it (`shake`).
- **Standing off**: a formation that stands off to shoot halts where its shooters reach, but never further than 0.8 of its sight of the foe's line at its torches (`getHalt`, `STAND_OFF`), reckoned anew as the light changes: in the dark or the murk it shoots at what it sees, nearer than two fronts if need be.
- **Sieges**: what walls add to the odds a storm asks for tells less the later the garrison sees the stormers (`getWallsOdds`): in full as they are seen 200 m off (`WALLS_SEEN`), down to half (`DARK_WALLS`), at night 0.6 of it, so 4 to one with no breach becomes 2.8. The besiegers' general reckons and rehearses the storm at those odds, and the engines batter only to the breaches they ask for: a siege at night is stormed where by day it is blockaded.
- **Deployment**: the attackers form up no further off than three sights from the defenders' ground (`APPROACH`, `getApproach`): 360 m at night. Ambushers lie as near as the column cannot see them (`findLairs`): in the dark or the murk the open hides them beyond its sight of troops lying still, 60 m at night, and trees let them lie at 30 m.
- **Cost**: the side's sight is reckoned once a round, the battalion's own at every look, a distance each; the light of a battle's tick is reckoned once (`remember`), so a clear day costs nothing.

## Energy

A battalion's `energy` is 1 fresh to 0 spent, and it strikes and stands with `0.5 + energy ÷ 2` of its strength. Every tick it spends by what it does (`EXERTION`, `tire`): marching 1/900 (gone in two hours), building 1/600, shooting 1/450, fighting at grips 1/225 (half an hour), running, at a charge or in flight, 1/150; standing idle wins back 1/450, an hour from spent to fresh. Running is going faster than 1.5 times its pace (`RUNNING`). Slopes and mud tire by taking longer, with no rule of their own. A short approach costs a few hundredths, so what it rewards is fresh reserves, latecomers and the side that waits for the foe.

## Morale

Morale is a battalion's own, 0 to 100. A side starts at a morale by the odds, its states' stability and the distance from base; a regiment that comes up later starts at its own, by its state and with itself in the odds, not at the side's; it forms up behind its side, kept on the field as a body, a garrison's within its walls (`enlist`). Then it moves only by what happens to it:

- **losses** and **hits in the flank and rear** shake it, the less the steadier the unit: all its men lost would cost 300 morale (`MORALE_LOSS`), so a battalion breaks at about a fifth of them lost;
- **odds at hand**: each round it counts friends and foes in order within 2.95 battalion fronts: badly outnumbered it loses heart, with the upper hand it takes heart; battalions marching off count as neither, and keep no broken troops running;
- **left alone**, with no foe near and not hit, it recovers;
- a friend that **breaks, surrenders or is destroyed** nearby shakes it, a foe's heartens it;
- **hunger**: a garrison cut off takes heart from nothing and loses morale every round, see the siege phases.

Under 30 a battalion is **shaken**: it holds and defends but does not attack. At 15 it **breaks** and runs. Broken troops run only while a foe in order is within 4.5 battalion fronts or shots land on them; then they halt, recover and **rally** at 35, as long as their side still fights: line troops and riders join the reserve, shooters and engines keep their role, as often as they get away.

A battalion about to break, under 20 (`YIELD`), with foes at hand and no way off the field by any edge, round deep water and walls and past no foe in order (`hasWayOut`; fliers and the ships of a land battle always have one), **surrenders** instead (`isCornered`). It is `surrendered`: it stands, still in the way of all (`present` in `act`), but is nobody's target, strikes no blow and is left out of the power and the troops a side has. Its men are **captured**, a category of their own: neither fallen (`casualties`, `getLosses`) nor left (`getTroops`), but `captured` (`getCaptured`); the outcome, the rehearsals and the general count both as lost (`getLost`). Its side holding the field frees them at the end. Before that it takes up arms again (`isFreed`, logged `freed`) at the start of a round in which nobody holds it any more: the foe has turned to run or laid down its arms, or friends in order stand within 2.95 fronts of it and no foe in order does; it stands again as one rallied, at a morale of 35 at least. Not while its own side runs or has given up, and never a battalion told by hand to surrender (`block.parole`). It shakes friends and heartens foes nearby as a rout does.

## Screen

The battle screen ([battle-screen.ts](../../src/controllers/political/battle/battle-screen.ts)) draws the field once ([draw-battle-scene.ts](../../src/renderers/battle/draw-battle-scene.ts)) and moves only the view box: the camera causes no redrawing.

- **Layers**: the stage (`#battleStage`) stacks the scene SVG (`#battleScene`), the weather canvas, the overlay SVG and the summary. Each layer of the scene is a group with its own id, listed bottom to top at the head of draw-battle-scene.ts: the ground (`#battleTerrain`, its parts `#battleTerrain<Part>`) and walls are drawn once; the works, battalions, shots and clashes are redrawn as the battle goes and are the layers the animation export records (`MOVING_LAYERS`). The look switches hide layers by id and the marks spread over several layers by class.
- **Camera** ([battle-camera.ts](../../src/renderers/battle/battle-camera.ts)): pure, troops and field in, a view box out. _Follow_ frames every battalion on the field (the fled and the destroyed left out) with two battalion fronts round them, widened to the scene's proportions, at least six fronts wide and inside the scene; the view eases to it over about a second of screen time, whatever the playback speed, and ignores framings that moved less than 4%. _Whole field_ shows the field and the ground drawn round it (`SURROUND`), an even margin on every side: the screen and the exports take the scene's shape (`VIEW_ASPECT`), not the field's 5:3. The wheel or a pinch zooms at the pointer and a right or middle drag, or a finger, pans: the camera is then _manual_ until a double-click on the ground returns it to the mode chosen in Configure Battle. The exports run the same camera, so the same battle gives the same views.
- **Selection**: a click picks a battalion, a left drag a box of them, a click on the ground drops the pick (`selected`, by key). Picked battalions get a light outline. Before the start a drag of a picked battalion moves all the picked ones and a handle just before their front turns them about their middle; both are `move` and `turn` changes. A change of the armies that makes battalions of another size drops them (`prepare`): those are other battalions on another field. After it a right or a shift click gives the picked a `task`: the ground a move, a foe an attack, a broken foe a pursuit, a friend protection; a click on a picked battalion opens a menu of the tasks on their own spots (`openTasks`), each picked battalion taking the ones it can. Picked battalions show their task at once, before they act on it (a dashed line to the goal, a solid one to the foe, a dotted one to the friend), else where they go and whom they fight; the range shows only on hover.
- **Sizes**: battalions are true to size; outlines, health lines, flags, icons (16 points at most), labels, fire lines, missiles and clash marks keep their size on screen, sized anew when the zoom changes by more than 3% (`setView`).
- **Overlay** ([draw-battle-overlay.ts](../../src/renderers/battle/draw-battle-overlay.ts)): the title and clock, the scale bar of the view in sight and the North arrow sit on an SVG above the scene that does not move with the view. Each part is drawn anew only when it changes, so the animated SVG export records it as switches.
- **Rendering**: each part of the drawing is a switch applied by a `<style>` the scene carries into its exports (`setLook`), never by rebuilding the field. Side colors are red and blue, or the colors of each side's leading state (the one with the most troops), red and blue again when the two are closer than 25 in Lab.
- **Ground** ([draw-battle-ground.ts](../../src/renderers/battle/draw-battle-ground.ts)): the paper is each node's map biome colour made pale (lightness 93, a third of its chroma, 14 at most), one smoothed image of the node grid. Props are cartographic glyphs on a jittered grid of spots 11 points apart, a glyph some 6 points across whatever the field's size, so a big field has no more of them than a small one; every kind is one path, and nothing of them reaches the battle. What stands on a spot is picked by its ground: scree below a cliff, shingle on the strand of a sea or lake (reeds in wetland), trees in the woods by the tree entries of the biome's relief icon pool (`icons`), reeds in the bogs, rocks on slopes steeper than 0.15, a few rocks on cold desert and tundra and crevasses on glacier, else an entry of the biome's pool on a share of spots by the root of its `iconsDensity`, trees there seldom. Reeds stand along river banks, a sand strand under the shore line. Lakes show their kind: a white salt crust round a salt lake, ice and lava with a rim and no strand, a pale cracked bed where a lake dried up; the others are open water. Custom biomes get the props of their pools, the same icon names. Seeded by the site, so a field always looks the same. Woods and bogs are pale fills that fade out over a node or two at their edges (`getSoftMask`: their node grid blurred twice, a mask image in which they fade in from a quarter to three quarters); their trees and reeds are props, thinning out towards the edge as it fades, a few standing beyond it. They hide with the woods as well as with the props, so a field has one set of ground marks and no patterns.
- **Towns** ([draw-battle-town.ts](../../src/renderers/battle/draw-battle-town.ts)): a burg is drawn as rooftops. The roads within its outline are its main streets, meeting in a market at its point, and a gate no road comes in by gets a street of its own. Voronoi cells of seeds paired along the streets and thrown loosely over the rest are its blocks, yards with houses along their edges, kept within the block, made shallower where a full one would not fit; a house laid over another covers it as seen from above: tile roofs in the middle, thatch more often toward the edge, some slate. Blocks are 13 points across or an 8.5th of the radius, whichever is larger, so any burg has about a hundred. Its streets and market are paved like the roads outside (`ROAD`), houses kept back from their edge, and its ground is drawn at 0.9 opacity. The roads are drawn over the towns and the market square over the roads (`battleTerrainPlazas`), so a road runs through the gate and into the square unbroken. A walled burg is clipped to its walls with a walk kept clear inside them; an open one thins out toward its edge and leaves water bare. Seeded by its outline, decoration only.
- **Light** ([draw-battle-light.ts](../../src/renderers/battle/draw-battle-light.ts)): the ground is shaded by the sun of the moment and the sky of the weather, the tint following the time at once, the shade baked anew once the sun moved a minute's way, no more often than every 0.4 s while it drifts, at once when the hour, the weather or the look changed (`drawLight`). A WebGL2 fragment shader bakes the shade from the relief's surface (heights, and slopes read on each side of a cliff) into a JPEG image twice its samples across, laid over the ground in `#battleLightShade`, under the labels and the battalions, and multiplied into it, so the exports carry it: a slope is darkened by how much less light it gets than level ground, from the sun, in the soft shadows the heights cast towards it (marched over the surface, cliffs included, heights steepened three times at most), and from the cartographer's sky in the north-west; the darkest shade is a sky blue. Slopes are steepened as for the relief's shade, a low field shaded fainter. Cloud takes the sun's share of the light (rain 75%, snow 85%, fog 90%, downpour 95%), leaving the soft north-west light; at night the sky's light alone shows the relief, fainter. A tint over the whole plan, battalions, shots and clashes too (`#battleLight` above them, the pointer going through to them; multiplied; a moving layer of the exports, laid on the canvas of a GIF frame under the overlay) goes from white by day to a dull blue at night, grey under cloud, golden while the sun is low and rosy in the glow after it sets and before it rises. Without WebGL2 the relief keeps its own soft shadow from the north-west (`getShade`) under the tint. The relief shade switch hides the baked shade, never the tint. At night every battalion with its torches lit (`isLit`: all but ambushers lying in wait) carries its light (`drawLamps`, `LAMP`): a faint warm glow as far as it sees past its edge, gentle where many overlap, where it is shown, laid over the tint inside the light layer, so the tint lifts round it; it shows as the light fails, fully at night. A GIF frame draws the tint and the glows on a canvas of their own and multiplies it in. With the time of day light switched off (`light`) the plan is lit as a map: no tint, the shade by the north-west sky alone.
- **Weather** ([draw-battle-weather.ts](../../src/renderers/battle/draw-battle-weather.ts)): rain, snow, fog and heat are a WebGL fragment shader on a canvas over the scene, drawn every frame while there is weather to show and the canvas is on screen: light slate streaks in three depths slanted by the wind, a pixel wide whatever the screen, faint splash rings and a light cool veil, denser in a downpour; fog in banks that roll with the wind, change shape as they go and thicken and thin, with wisps streaming over them, thicker towards the edges of the view; snow as flakes that drift and sway, each with a soft grey rim that shows it on pale ground; heat as a warm shimmering haze. Soaked ground is drawn with the field (`#battleMud`, hidden unless the scene is `battleMuddy`, `getMudMarkup`): blotches of darker wet earth where the ground lies wet, and in them on open level ground puddles, the more the lower it lies, each a halo of wet earth, the water and a glint; placed by the node, so the same field is always soaked alike. Frozen ground is a pale frost over the field (`#battleFrost`) and icy rivers (`.battleRiver`), shown when the scene is `battleIcy`. A rendering switch, on the screen only: the exports leave it out.
- **Log**: a dialog of its own beside the screen (`toggleLog`), drawn anew from the first entry that changed (`renderLog`). It opens with the tactic of each side (`noteTactics` after every drawing up, before the start too), so a tactic given by hand writes no line of its own. A siege the besiegers draw off from before a garrison out in force is logged `raised`, not `broken` (`battle.raised`), and pauses and keyframes like it. Morale clicks in a row make one change before the start, where the troops are lined up anew; in the battle each click stands as it was made, so the record is what was applied. The log file carries the approach (`axis`), so a battle played again comes from the same side whichever regiment is first.
- **Tips**: the tip of what is under a pointer that stays still is shown anew every frame (`retip`), so it follows the troops, the camera and the numbers that change under it.
- **Shots at works**: an engine that batters the walls of a besieged burg (`getWallShot`: the nearest point of the nearest solid stretch) or knocks down the foe's wagons (`Works.getBattered`) shells it, half a cycle after its shells at troops, so the walls and wagons are seen to be struck before they fall (`getWorkShots`).
- **Movement arrows**: the screen keeps each battalion's place at every round boundary since the last key moment (the start, the moment play was resumed, a report keyframe) and, when the option is on (off by default), draws the way since, smoothed, while paused and in report keyframes. A battalion that moved less than its depth gets none.
- **Playback**: the step is a tick or a round, either way; a step back replays the battle from its start to there (`startAnew`), with every change made on the way, those after it kept to come again: the battle keeps per-battle caches outside its object, so a copy of it would not go on the same. The player pauses after a tick that logged an event chosen to pause at: the first clash (`clash`, when `battle.engaged` is set), a rout, a surrender, a breach, a side broken (`broken`, when its phase turns to running or surrender).
- **Exports**, from Configure Battle: a GIF or an animated SVG ([battle-animation.ts](../../src/controllers/political/battle/battle-animation.ts)), or a battle report ([battle-report.ts](../../src/controllers/political/battle/battle-report.ts)): one offline HTML page with the battle summary and a keyframe at the start, after each round with an event of the kinds chosen to pause at (the first clash, routs and a side broken if none), and at the end, twelve at most, those with the most routs, surrenders and losses kept (`Keyframes.select`). A keyframe is the field framed by the export camera, with the overlay, the arrows since the previous one and the log lines between them. Both replay the battle from its start.

**Options** are kept per browser in `options.app.battle` (view, rendering, playback, simulation, export), never in the map file. The simulation numbers (`BattleSettings` in [battle-options.ts](../../src/data/battle-options.ts): the most and the fewest battalions, the longest battle, the lull, how long an order stands, how far ahead the generals think) are copied into a battle when it is set up (`battle.settings`); the model reads them from the battle, never from the options. A change applies to the next battle, or to the current one before its start, which is then set up anew. The battle log stores them, so a loaded log replays with the numbers it was fought with; a log without them takes the defaults.

## Tuning and checks

Tests switch works off (`WORKS.on` in `works.ts`) to compare with the battle without them: off, it plays exactly as before works came. Measure balance over several army sizes and all eight directions of attack: a battle is one fixed outcome, so a single size tells nothing. Measure on a hill, behind a ford, between woods and on a shore too: open ground alone hid a defender who waded into the ford he was winning at. The directions matter: a diagonal attack has other room than a straight one. Watch a battle with the Battles diagnostics on (Advanced options): they draw every battalion's mission, its place and its target over the plan, and each side's tactic and stance.
